==========================================================================================
1. SUMMARY
==========================================================================================

- The trial bought four things: a source that passes the known-transaction test; a
  validated universe of about 180,543 stratum-P tokens; a fill and fee model that
  reproduces executed swaps exactly; and measured unit costs for every column group of
  the per-token table. All four are complete enough to buy against.

- Of 2,052.5 credits used, about 889 produced data and about 1,163 (57%) were lost to
  five events: one runaway query (840), two watchdog cancellations (164), one query
  that ran on an empty column (85), and two oversized result fetches (73). Each loss
  produced a rule that is now enforced in the runner. Section 2 lists them.

- The most decision-relevant datapoints, in the builder's judgement:
    (a) Graduates are traded by bots within one second, and the price at a 15 to 240
        minute lag sits far below the graduation price: median 55% / 12% / 7% of it at
        15 / 60 / 240 minutes in June 2025, 32% / 15% / 10% on a post-BOOST day. [M]
    (b) In the calibration week, a token bought at any of the three lags and held seven
        days was above its entry price 5-7% of the time; the mean price ratio was 0.54,
        0.73 and 0.84 before any cost. One week, one era, mid prices. [M]
    (c) Trading cost per leg went from about 30 bps in June 2025 to 112-119 bps from
        October 2025 on. Round trip about 0.6% then, about 2.3% now. [M]
    (d) 89% of graduates lose 80% of their pool reserves within seven days, so reserve
        drain cannot label a rug. The label is the aligned set's selling (RUG-A). [M]
    (e) The aligned set is one wallet on the median token. It holds anything at entry on
        34% of tokens, and on 22% of those it sells essentially all of it in a week. [M]
    (f) Wallet counts are mostly exchanges and hubs. The largest group of early buyers
        sharing a funder has a median of 142 wallets; restricted to funders that can
        link, 3 to 9. [M]
    (g) Seven of the ten pre-registered gate checks have no discriminating power on
        pump.fun graduates: they pass by construction or flag almost everything. [M]

- Value of the data collected (Section 5): replacement cost $29-38 in credits ($12-17 for
  the productive part); it has already removed roughly 30,000 credits ($420-560) of
  spend that the pre-measurement plan would have incurred; its decision value is
  bounded by the $2,000 bankroll.

- Paid build (Section 6): Analyst with the ruled cut is 6,300-10,900 credits, two to
  three months, about $150-225 if the monthly price is near $75 [A]. One Plus month is
  19,900-25,900 credits of ruled scope against 25,000, about $349-400 [A]. On a $2,000
  bankroll the purchase is 7.5% to 20% of capital. It pays for itself if it prevents
  one blind deployment cycle, or if it finds a gated net edge of 2-5% per seven-day
  cycle that persists for a few cycles. The data in hand leans toward the first.

2.3 What the losses imply for the paid month [D]
  The same failure classes at paid scale would cost more, not less. The controls are in
  place: expected cost is a hard cap for any unproven query, every new query runs at
  one-day scope first, every input column is null-counted per era, and results are not
  fetched when only the cost is wanted. One caution stands: Dune's in-flight cost
  counter moves in steps of up to 56 credits, so cancellation cannot stop a fast
  overrun. The one-day-first rule is the real protection.

3.1 Source fitness
  Datapoints [M]
    - Dune passes the known-transaction round-trip on four chain specimens: routed swap
      0 outer / 6 inner instructions exactly as on chain; decoded lp, protocol and
      creator fee equal an independent decode of the chain bytes, 4 of 4.
    - BigQuery's public Solana set failed the same test (no inner instructions, empty
      logs). Flipside is dead.
    - Fee proportions hold across 26 million events on one day: LP exactly 25.0 bps,
      protocol exactly 5.0 bps.
    - 18% of PumpSwap pools on 2026-09-01 are not SOL-quoted and carry half the event
      count (13.4M of 26.3M).
    - Curated tables lag raw tables by up to a day.
    - Dune's buy-event decoder pins an older layout: no virtual_quote_reserves.
  Extrapolation
    - The source question is closed for M0. For M1 (live), a one-day lag on curated
      tables means Dune cannot be the live store.
    - Any SOL-denominated figure must filter on quote mint, or it is wrong by orders of
      magnitude. P_alt (non-SOL pools) is a population the same size as P by flow and is
      outside M0.

3.2 Universe
  Datapoints [M]
    - Stratum P over the window: 97,613 tokens to June 2026 by the complete-to-pool
      rule, 82,930 from July to September 2026 by the migration event; about 180,543.
    - The two admission sources agree to 0.04% on the overlap month and exactly
      (1,154 of 1,154) on 2026-09-01. Complete-to-pool lag: p50 1 s, p90 2 s.
    - Monthly P graduates run 2,500 to 11,400 before BOOST; June 2026 7,212, July 2026
      21,666.
    - Graduation rate: 0.72% of launches pre-BOOST week, 3.39% post-BOOST week (4.7x).
    - Stratum R (pre-2025-03-20, Raydium): 339 of 339 matched on a sample day, one
      signer. Stratum N: 197 admitted on a sample day across three venues; CLMM
      coverage on Dune is stale.
    - Migration calls outnumber migrations 2,693 to 1,209 on one day: bots race a
      permissionless instruction.
  Extrapolation
    - About 46% of the P universe graduated in the last three months of the window.
      Any pooled statistic is dominated by the post-BOOST era unless split by era,
      which the schema does.
    - 180,543 tokens are fewer independent observations than they look: see blocks, 3.9.

3.3 Census and organic activity
  Datapoints [M]
    - Every graduate is traded within seven days: 100.0% in both eras. The original
      census metric carries no information and was withdrawn.
    - Organic takers, definition v1 (not creator, migrator or same-slot buyer), one
      post-BOOST week of 7,236 tokens: p10 18, p50 420, p90 3,202. First organic swap
      within 60 seconds on 7,221 of 7,236.
    - Definition v2 (adds wallet age 24 h and not bot-shaped): p50 207; 127 tokens with

    - June 2025 week, v1: p50 1,402 takers, first organic swap at 0 seconds at the

  Extrapolation

      behind it on about 97% of tokens. "Be early" is not an available strategy at any
      pre-registered lag; the question M0 can answer is whether being late and
      selective pays.

3.4 The safety gate, check by check
  Datapoints [M]
    - S1 mint authority, S2 freeze authority: 5 of 5 sample mints reconciled to chain;
      differential test 7 of 7 on stratum N. On P: revoked in the creation transaction
      on 236 of 236 (day) and 1,523 of 1,523 (week). PASS by construction.
    - S3 extensions, S4 transfer fee: 10 of 10 reconciled. One live failure found, a
      500 bps transfer tax on a stratum-N mint. On P, only the metadata pointer.
    - S5 LP: burned by the migration itself on P. PASS by construction.
    - S6 top-10 holders: ledger reconciled to chain supply to the lamport and to chain
      balances exactly on a test mint. Top-10 share of supply at G = 240 min, week:
      median 12.9%, 90th percentile 24.4%. One day: median 14.6%, 99th 48.9%.
      Median holders at entry: 142.
    - S7 linked supply: median 0%, 90th percentile 0.6%, 99th 26.7%, maximum 91%.
    - S8 bundle at creation: 43 of 1,523 tokens (2.8%).
    - S9b round-trip share of day-one volume: p50 0.89; 1,116 of 1,154 flagged. Vacuous
      at the registered 30%. S9a flags 5% by construction.
    - S10 sellable within 30 minutes: 1,151 of 1,154, and 1,573 of 1,573 in the June
      week. Near-vacuous.
  Extrapolation
    - On stratum P the gate reduces to three checks with any spread: S6 concentration,
      S7 linked supply, S8 bundles. S1-S5 pass by construction, S9 and S10 were demoted
      to features. H1's recall will be decided by those three plus RUG-A's definition.
    - At the pre-registered thresholds, on the calibration week [M]: S6 above 40% on 28
      of 1,523 tokens (1.8%); S7 above 15% on 26 (1.7%); S8 on 43 (2.8%). A gate that
      flags at most 6.4% of tokens can only matter if those are disproportionately the
      ones that dump.
    - The gate's untapped power is in strata R and N, where authorities and transfer
      taxes are live.

3.5 Fill model and fees
  Datapoints [M]
    - Constant-product fill with pre-swap reserves reproduces base_out on 10 of 10
      executed buys and quote_out on 10 of 10 executed sells to 0.0000%, both eras.
      Post-BOOST pools carry a 17.585 SOL virtual quote reserve; omitting it gives
      errors of 0.3% to 23%.
    - Trader cost per leg, median: 119 bps (2025-10-15), 115 bps (2026-04-15),
      112 bps (2026-09-01). Creator fee median 82 / 60 / 31 bps. An unnamed leg grows:
      residual p90 0 / 30 / 53 bps.
    - June 2025 week: buy cost median 29.9 bps, sell 30.0 bps, creator fee 5 bps,
      residual 0.
    - Pool depth at G = 240 min, June week: median 23.4 SOL of quote reserve, 10th
      percentile 19.5, 90th 105.9.
  Extrapolation
    - Cost per leg roughly quadrupled between June and October 2025. A result from the
      early part of the window does not carry to the current fee regime without
      re-pricing; the per-era cost columns exist for that.
    - Round trip at current fees is about 2.3% before price impact. A $20 ticket
      against 23 SOL of depth moves the price by roughly half a percent, so fees
      dominate impact at this ticket size. [D]
    - The fill model is exact, so simulated fills carry no model error; the remaining
      error is in choosing the block and in the unnamed fee leg, which is stored.

3.6 What happens to price and reserves after graduation
  Datapoints [M]
    - Price at lag G divided by graduation price (bot-layer markup):
        post-BOOST day, 1,154 tokens:  G15 p50 0.32   G60 p50 0.15   G240 p50 0.10
        June 2025 week, 1,573 tokens:  G15 p50 0.55   G60 p50 0.12   G240 p50 0.07
      Upper tail: p90 2.7-3.0 at G15, 1.2-1.4 at G240.
    - Price 24 hours after graduation over graduation price, June week: median 0.056.
    - Quote reserves relative to graduation: post-BOOST day p50 0.17 at 1 h, 0.07 at
      24 h, 0.05 at 7 d; 1,031 of 1,154 (89%) down 80% or more within 7 days. June
      week p50 0.23 at 7 d.
    - Depth-floor ratio for a $20 ticket: p50 0.93; 2 of 1,154 below the 20% floor.
  Extrapolation
    - The graduation price is a spike, not a level. The registered expectation that the
      bot layer is the largest cost was wrong in sign at the median: the lagged entry
      is cheaper than graduation on about two thirds to nine tenths of tokens.
    - Reserve drain is the base rate of a graduate's first week and cannot define a
      rug. This is why RUG-A is the sole outcome variable.

3.7 Price paths after entry, calibration week (June 2025, 1,573 tokens)
  Descriptive only. Calibration slice; mid price to mid price; no fills, no costs, no
  gate. One week, pre-BOOST. This is not the H4 result.
  Datapoints [M]
                                   Entry at G15   Entry at G60   Entry at G240
    Price at 7 d / entry, median       0.16           0.60           0.80
    Price at 7 d / entry, mean         0.54           0.73           0.84
    Share of tokens above entry        5.3%           5.6%           7.2%
    Peak within 7 d / entry, median    1.36           1.23           1.18
    Share reaching 2x at any time      29.8%          22.6%          20.7%
    Peak within 24 h / entry, median   1.32           1.18           1.11
    Max drawdown within 7 d, median    92%            74%            46%
  Extrapolation
    - Buying every graduate and holding seven days lost 16% to 46% of capital on average
      in this week before costs. The later the entry, the smaller the loss, because
      most of the fall has already happened.
    - The whole distribution is the left tail plus a thin right tail (99th percentile
      4.6x to 4.9x). For a gate to turn the mean positive it has to select from the 5-7%
      that hold their price, not merely avoid the worst.
    - One fifth to three tenths of tokens trade at twice the entry price at some point
      in the week. That is a statement about paths, not a strategy: it is only
      capturable with an exit rule defined in advance, and the 24-hour peak columns
      exist so that a time-stop can be tested without look-ahead.
    - If the full window looks like this week, the expected M0 verdict on naive
      expectancy is NO at every lag, and the value of M0 is in knowing that and in
      whether the gated subset differs.

3.8 The aligned set and the RUG-A input
  Datapoints [M]
    - Set = creator + wallets the creator funded within 24 h + creation-slot bundle
      wallets, restricted to wallets that ever held the token. Median size 1; 90th
      percentile 3; 414 of 1,523 tokens have more than one member; one token has 1,391.
    - The earlier definition (every recipient of creator SOL) gave a median of 28 and a
      minimum of 5, all fee, tip and rent accounts. Struck by ruling.
    - Set holds nothing at G = 240 min on 1,000 of 1,523 tokens (66%).
    - Of the 523 that hold: 342 sold none in 7 days; 26 sold under half; 19 sold
      50-99.9%; 114 sold 99.9% or more; 22 bought more.
    - On a post-BOOST day a few tokens have sets of about 3,800 wallets; the median is
      still 1.
  Extrapolation
    - On two thirds of tokens the insiders are already out before any registered
      entry. RUG-A can only fire on the remaining third, and does fire, by any
      plausible threshold, on roughly 7.5% of all tokens (114 of 1,523). [D]
    - The sell-fraction distribution is bimodal (nothing or everything), so the choice
      of threshold X will barely move the label. That is a favourable property for a
      pre-registered outcome variable.
    - Fee-share recipients (c07b) are outside the set by ruling, which can only lower
      measured recall.

3.9 Funders, actor collapse, blocks, factories
  Datapoints [M]
    - Fan-out on Solana SOL transfers, 12 days: 16 of 30 active labelled exchange
      wallets paid 15 or fewer recipients; about 15,300 unlabelled senders paid more
      than 1,000. Fan-out alone does not classify. Ruled: label first, 400 provisional.
    - Member funders found inside the scanned window: 63% of members, 92% of creators
      (1,401 of 1,523).
    - Set states at 400: 1,024 solo, 236 independent, 55 collapsed, 208 unresolved
      (14%); post-BOOST day 35% unresolved.
    - Multi-wallet sets that resolve: 1,426 wallets become 737 actors at 400 (943 at 32,
      668 at 8,192).
    - Funding to first acquisition: median 886 s per member; 199 of 2,683 within 10 s.
      Measured under the earlier definition (last inflow); the ruled one (first inflow)
      is not re-measured.
    - Blocks, creator-wallet fallback: 1,287 for 1,523 tokens (0.845); post-BOOST day
      871 for 1,087 (0.801). Largest block 12 and 16 tokens. Threshold moves it by
      under 3%.
    - Factory test: among senders with 100 or more recipients that then traded, 130 had
      under 10% of them on one token and 84 had 100% on one token; 20 in between. All
      12 labelled exchanges in that group are in the first cluster.
  Extrapolation
    - Effective sample size is about 80-85% of the token count. The 180,543-token
      universe is roughly 145,000 to 153,000 blocks if the ratio holds. [D] Power is
      not the constraint for pooled cells; it may be for era-by-lag-by-gate cells.
    - The factory class is real and separable where there is enough evidence. It will
      matter most for the early-buyer groups, where hubs dominate.
    - One set in seven (one in three post-BOOST) cannot be collapsed with a four-day
      funding lookback. Lengthening the lookback costs credits one-for-one in days
      scanned; the unresolved rate is the price of the current design.

        entries at G15:   mean ratio 0.54   expected loss about $920
        entries at G60:   mean ratio 0.73   expected loss about $540
        entries at G240:  mean ratio 0.84   expected loss about $320
    Round-trip cost adds $12 (June 2025 fees) to $46 (current fees) per deployment.
    If these figures hold outside one week, the data already in hand is worth roughly
    one avoided cycle, $330 to $970, to anyone who would otherwise have deployed. It is
    worth nothing in dollars to someone who would not have. It is one week; treat the
    figure as an order of magnitude.
    Hard ceiling: no finding can be worth more than the capital it protects plus the
    edge it finds, and the capital is $2,000.

6.4 The builder's read
    - As a trading investment on $2,000, the purchase is hard to justify on expected
      profit: the outlay is a tenth of the bankroll and the data in hand leans NO.
    - As loss insurance it clears its cost if it stops one blind cycle.
    - As infrastructure it is cheap: under a quarter of a cent per token row, for a
      table that the calibration week shows can be built correctly, and whose queries
      and controls carry into M1.
    - Analyst against Plus: Analyst is cheaper in cash and slower, and under the cut it
      leaves H2 unanswered, which the crossover memo already treats as presumptively
      dead. Plus buys the whole ruled scope in one month and removes the public-table
      and export-rate questions, at about twice the cash. The ruling's condition
      (month-to-month at about $400 or less) is the right test. If only the gate and
      the naive expectancy are wanted, Analyst with the cut delivers both.
    - The cheapest thing that would sharpen this section is free: 267 trial credits
      remain until 2026-10-06, and the 3.7 statistics could be repeated on one
      post-BOOST calibration-style week of event columns (about 35-50 credits) to see
      whether the price-path picture holds in the current era. That week would have to
      be chosen inside the calibration slice or declared burned for evaluation. Not
      run; it needs a ruling.
